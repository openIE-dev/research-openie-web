## Everyday Analogy & Why It Matters

Faster attention kernels took ten months to ship and speculative decoding took two years. Beyond engineering work, listed companies gate releases around quarterly earnings targets and multi-year server depreciation. Once open algorithms make raw computation cheap on older chips, sellers charge mainly for the pain of switching away—and automation tools erase that pain.

Between late 2022 and late 2024, benchmark AI inference prices fell two hundred eighty times while hardware cost and chip energy efficiency each improved only about twofold, leaving a roughly hundred-forty-fold gain driven by software, utilization, and smaller models. Across seventy-four frontier data centers, one utilized accelerator hour costs between eighty-three cents and two-and-a-half dollars, and plug electricity is under twelve percent of that bill. Amortizing thirty-three-thousand-dollar machine capital and smoothing quarterly earnings sets deployment speed.

Auditing public regulatory filings bounds supplier-funded chip revenue below twenty percent at the leading chipmaker and shows major tech platforms paying out of operating cash, while exposing heavy leverage at specialized clouds whose purchase backlogs exceed eleven times annual revenue. When automated porting tools let buyers move workloads onto open methods and existing hardware without rewriting software stacks by hand, waiting stops paying and deployed prices snap downward toward the capability frontier.

## Inside the Mechanism & Candid Limits

Public research halves the compute needed to reach a fixed language-model score roughly every eight months, and open-weight models on a single consumer graphics card match frontier scores within six to twelve months. Yet commercial products routinely wait ten to twenty-four months to ship published speedups like fast attention and speculative decoding. Just as factories took decades to reorganize around individual electric motors, enterprises today face real adoption drag—official surveys read in eight original languages show only eighteen to twenty percent of American and European firms using AI by early 2026, while listed executives admit delaying worthwhile projects to smooth quarterly earnings. A bottom-up infrastructure model across seventy-four frontier sites finds median capital near thirty-three thousand three hundred dollars and eight hundred eighty facility watts per chip.

Over four-to-six-year server lives, raw plug electricity is just one-point-five to eleven-point-eight percent of an accelerator hour—hardware capital dominates eight to seventy times over. Because electricity is under one eighth of task cost, cheaper power alone barely moves dollar prices. Cutting the chip time a task occupies slashes capital cost directly—explaining why two-hundred-eighty-fold inference price drops outpaced two-fold hardware improvements by a factor of roughly one hundred forty-one across just under two years.
