From orbit to a hand's width

A whole planet of 6,371 km radius, generated from a seed as you approach it. Every vertex is an exact integer point. The GPU only ever sees small numbers: each tile is drawn relative to its own centre, offset from the camera by a difference computed exactly.

Companion figure to Section 2.2 of A Planet You Can Take Apart. Drag to turn the planet, scroll or use the slider to change altitude.

What this is. A first renderer: terrain from 3D integer noise (relief of about ±5 km), level of detail from a quadtree on each face of a cube, no atmosphere, no caves yet. Colours are a view of elevation and latitude only. Tiles are built on the CPU in JavaScript as you move, so new ground takes a moment to sharpen.

Try this

Press Stand on the ground, wait for the tiles to sharpen, then tick creep. The camera moves 5 mm a frame and the ground glides smoothly.

Now switch positions to planet-centred 32-bit. At 6,371 km from the centre a 32-bit float can only say where something is to the nearest half metre, so the ground jumps in half-metre steps instead of gliding, and nearby vertices snap to a coarse grid.
