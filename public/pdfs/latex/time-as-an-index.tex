\section{0. Overview}\label{overview}

If a future state of a physical or computational system could be queried
directly like an indexed row in a relational table, sequential
brute-force search --- cryptographic key recovery, hash preimage
inversion, and proof-of-work puzzle solving --- would cease to protect
secrets, because the target witness was never absent, only unindexed.
This study tests that premise across computational complexity theory,
quantum retrocausality, physical relaxation hardware, cryptographic game
reductions, formal verification in Lean 4 (\texttt{31} kernel-checked
theorems with zero \texttt{sorry} axioms), deterministic numerical and
symbolic models (\texttt{seed\ =\ 20261122}), and three structured
public catalogs totaling \texttt{195} entries (\texttt{81} reported
temporal anomalies, \texttt{40} physical frequency-domain edge cases,
and \texttt{74} foundational paradoxes). No hardware rail or
alternating-current wall power meter was attached
(\texttt{board\_\allowbreak{}metered\ =\ false}).

The verdict splits cleanly along the boundary between formal closed
timelike curves (CTCs) and known laboratory physics. Inside Deutsch's
fixed-point closed-timelike-curve model, polynomial-time classical and
quantum computers both reach full polynomial-space computing power,
collapsing elliptic-curve signatures, symmetric block ciphers,
cryptographic hashes, proof-of-work puzzles, and prepare-and-measure
quantum key distribution (QKD) in a single fixed-point query while
sparing only information-theoretic schemes such as the one-time pad.
Outside formal time loops, no known physical process grants early
lookup: quantum retrocausal interpretations inherit the no-signalling
theorem, postselected teleportation loops pay an inverse-probability
repetition bill (\(\sim 1/p\), reaching \(1/d^2\) on \(d\)-state witness
circuits), analog dynamical solvers trade step count for exponential
energy fluctuations or exponential frequency precision, and every
physically realizable ``future index'' reduces to precomputation bounded
by Hellman's \(T \cdot M^2 = N^2\) tradeoff after an \(N\)-step fill
that salts and fresh challenges defeat before construction can start.

\textbf{In plain words}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Inside a mathematical time loop, almost every math-based lock
  opens at once.} If hardware could force a register sent into its own
  past to return unchanged, consistency alone would compel
  nondeterministic and polynomial-space answers to appear in polynomial
  gate count; only information-theoretic secrets such as a one-time pad
  survive.
\item
  \textbf{In known physics, nothing sends a readable answer backward in
  time.} Quantum delayed-choice and retrocausal models obey exact
  marginal no-signalling, postselection discards roughly \(1/p\) failed
  runs to keep one consistent run, and out of \texttt{81} audited
  temporal anomaly reports and \texttt{40} frequency-atlas anomalies,
  zero Grade A or Grade B cases survive as unexplained reception from
  the future.
\item
  \textbf{Every working ``lookup of tomorrow'' today is precomputation
  paid in advance.} Building a table moves the search earlier at fill
  cost \(N\), obeys \(T \cdot M^2 = N^2\), dissipates at least
  \(k_B T \ln 2\) per erased bit, and fails completely whenever a fresh
  salt or previous-block hash withholds the question until the moment of
  use.
\end{enumerate}

\section{\texorpdfstring{2. Formal Models of Time Loops in Computation
(\texttt{D-\allowbreak{}CTC\ =\ PSPACE}, \texttt{P-\allowbreak{}CTC\ =\ PP}, Unbounded
\(\Delta_2\))}{2. Formal Models of Time Loops in Computation (D-CTC = PSPACE, P-CTC = PP, Unbounded \textbackslash Delta\_2)}}\label{formal-models-of-time-loops-in-computation-d-ctc-pspace-p-ctc-pp-unbounded-delta_2}

Deutsch (1991) resolved the classical grandfather contradiction on
finite quantum registers by requiring kinematic self-consistency at the
boundary of a closed timelike curve. Let a chronology-respecting input
state \(\rho_{\text{in}}\) on \(\mathcal{H}_{\text{CR}}\) interact
through a joint unitary \(U\) with a causality-violating register
\(\rho_{\text{CTC}}\) on \(\mathcal{H}_{\text{CV}}\) that re-enters its
own past. Consistency requires \(\rho_{\text{CTC}}\) to be a fixed point
of the induced quantum channel (\texttt{Established} inside the model):

\[\rho_{\text{CTC}} = \mathrm{Tr}_{\text{CR}}\!\left[\, U \left(\rho_{\text{in}} \otimes \rho_{\text{CTC}}\right) U^\dagger \,\right], \qquad \rho_{\text{out}} = \mathrm{Tr}_{\text{CV}}\!\left[\, U \left(\rho_{\text{in}} \otimes \rho_{\text{CTC}}\right) U^\dagger \,\right].\]

Because every completely positive trace-preserving channel on a compact
convex state space of finite dimension possesses at least one
Schauder-Brouwer fixed point, no circuit triggers a logical
contradiction; instead, deterministic paradoxes become mixed states.
Critically, because \(\rho_{\text{CTC}}\) depends on
\(\rho_{\text{in}}\), the effective input-to-output map
\(\rho_{\text{in}} \mapsto \rho_{\text{out}}\) is \textbf{nonlinear} in
\(\rho_{\text{in}}\). That nonlinearity --- rather than quantum
superposition alone --- drives the computational jump across all formal
CTC models:

\begin{enumerate}

  causal re-evaluation of the chain that followed it.

\end{enumerate}

\[T \cdot M_{\text{tot}}^2 = N^2 \qquad \text{after a one-time table fill of } \Theta(N) \text{ evaluations.}\]

\(N = 2^{16} = 65{,}536\), \(N^{2/3} \approx 1{,}625.5\)), online lookup

exhaustive search upfront, refined in constant factors by Oechslin's
(2003) rainbow tables and proved optimal for generic random functions by
Fiat and Naor (2000), alongside Bennett's (1989) reversible pebbling
bounds. Salting with \(s\) random bits multiplies fill work by \(2^s\),
and binding a query to a fresh challenge (a post-request nonce or
previous block header hash) reduces precomputed table reuse to zero
(\texttt{0.0\%} across \texttt{200} fresh-block trials in \texttt{M7} vs
\texttt{34.0\%} single-table unsalted recovery).

\subsection{4.1 Quantum retrocausality and the postselection

Several foundational programmes describe quantum correlations using
future boundary conditions: the Aharonov-Bergmann-Lebowitz (1964)
two-state vector formalism (\texttt{TSVF}, \texttt{Established} as an
equivalent reformulation), Leifer and Pusey's (2017) theorem that any
\(\lambda\)-mediated time-symmetric ontology reproducing entanglement
must admit retrocausal correlations (\texttt{Established} under stated
assumptions), Price and Wharton's (2015) zigzag reading
(\texttt{Speculation} as new physics), Castagnoli's (2009--2025)
interpretive rule comparing quantum query counts to a classical learner
given half the solution bits (\texttt{Conjecture}), and Aharonov and
Elitzur's (\texttt{arXiv:2611.\allowbreak{}12971}, Nov 2026 preprint) two-branch
time-translation model in which postselected atomic re-excitation
survives only within the environment's radiation memory time --- roughly
\(1\text{ fs}\) in free space --- before demanding exponentially
decaying postselection probabilities (\texttt{Conjecture}). Likewise,
Ji, Lloyd, and Wilde (\emph{Phys. Rev.~Lett.} 136:230801, 2026) derived
the noisy \texttt{P-CTC} retrocausal capacity from max-information and
regularized Doeblin information while explicitly noting that standard
quantum mechanics permits zero backward signalling (\texttt{Established}
in \texttt{P-CTC}).

None of these frameworks yields an early lookup oracle because standard
quantum mechanics satisfies the \textbf{no-signalling theorem}: for any
bipartite or two-time state \(\rho_{12}\) and any future/remote
instrument \(\{E_b^y\}\) chosen freely via setting \(y\) satisfying
\(\sum_b E_b^y = I_2\), the earlier marginal outcome distribution is
strictly independent of \(y\):

\[P(a \mid x, y) = \mathrm{Tr}\!\left[(M_a^x \otimes I_2)\,\rho_{12}\right] = P(a \mid x).\]

Conditioning on a desired postselection outcome \(b = b^*\) with
probability \(p\) requires repeating the preparation roughly \(1/p\)
times (``anthropic computing'', Aaronson 2005). In the explicit unitary
witness-search model \texttt{M2b} (\texttt{3} to \texttt{6} bits,
Hilbert dimension \(d = 2^n \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}\)), \texttt{P-CTC}
Bell projection succeeds with exact probability
\(P_{\text{post}} = 1/d^2\), requiring \texttt{64}, \texttt{256},
\texttt{1,024}, and \texttt{4,096} expected repetitions --- strictly
costlier than classical \(d/2\) exhaustive guessing (\texttt{4} to
\texttt{32} queries).
