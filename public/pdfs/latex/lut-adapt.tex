\section{0. Overview}\label{overview}

This paper studies how a machine can turn repeated neural-model work into cheap table lookups without losing exact output parity or promoting unverified guesses into trusted rules. It reports software measurements, register-transfer-level (RTL) synthesis and cycle simulations, and labeled analytical energy estimates.

\textbf{In plain words}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Most inputs a deployed system sees are variations of patterns it has already solved, so paying full neural-network compute and memory traffic on every token wastes energy and bandwidth.
  \item We place a fast, reconfigurable 6-input truth-table (\texttt{LUT6}) lookup layer in front of streaming state-space and foundation models, and compile answers downward into lookup memory pages only after a strict check certifies them.
  \item When late-2026 hybrid and sparse Mixture-of-Experts (\texttt{MoE}) language models up to \texttt{34.66B} parameters stream 4-bit weights from NVMe (\texttt{Expert-AirMoL}), router-sparse expert \texttt{pread} plus zero-FLOP \texttt{LUT6} burst drafting cuts disk reads by up to \texttt{12.30$\times$} (\texttt{6.50$\times$} faster \texttt{tok/s}) in \texttt{2.53 GB} RAM (\texttt{3.83\%} of FP16) with zero change to output tokens; pairing the cascade with a deterministic verifier $C(z)$ beats standalone \texttt{35B} accuracy with \texttt{0.00\%} broken commits admitted and accelerates crystallized Wave-3 tasks by \texttt{35,590$\times$}.
\end{enumerate}

In \emph{Metered Commit Gates} [same house], Part II introduced \textbf{Mixture of Limits (MoL)}: try gears in decreasing order of pass probability per joule $p_i / c_i$ (Simon and Kadane, 1975), stop as soon as the task completion predicate $C(z) = 1$ holds, and never promote \texttt{ModelGenerated} output to \texttt{Deterministic}. A static Lookup gear, however, covers only the patterns written into it before deployment. Conversely, existing lookup-table neural networks --- LogicNets (Umuroglu et al., 2020), DiffLogic (Petersen et al., 2022), and Differentiable Weightless Neural Networks (Bacellar et al., 2024) --- bake wire connectivity into fixed FPGA routing, requiring minutes to hours of vendor place-and-route (\texttt{Vivado}) whenever the input distribution shifts.

This work closes the loop between the top and bottom of the cascade via \textbf{online downward \texttt{LUT6} compilation}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Rung 0 (Lookup --- Paged Bene\v{s} + \texttt{LUT6})}: Evaluates 64-bit truth-table \texttt{INIT} masks routed through a non-blocking $512 \times 512$ \textbf{Bene\v{s}--Copy--Bene\v{s}} multicast network and cached across $81 \times \texttt{RAMB36E2}$ dual-port block RAMs (\texttt{0} floating-point operations, \texttt{CPI = 1} cycle context switch in Verilator simulation).
  \item \textbf{Rung 1 (Formula / Stream --- \texttt{Mamba-3} Complex MIMO SSD)}: Handles misses that require sequence context via a constant-memory $O(1)$ recurrent state (\texttt{48 KB} pinned SRAM in our \texttt{14.3M}-parameter reference), avoiding $O(L)$ KV-cache growth.
  \item \textbf{Rung 2 (Topological Router --- \texttt{HC-SOINN})}: Maintains online prototype topology over latent embeddings, isolating regime boundaries and tripping a circuit breaker (\texttt{Refuse} / escalate) when unlabeled distribution shift arrives.
  \item \textbf{Rung 3 (Solver / Model LAST --- \texttt{JEPA-EBRM} \& \texttt{AirMoL} 4-Bit NVMe Streaming)}: Wakes deep latent energy-based verification or layer-streamed pretrained 2026 foundation models (\texttt{LiquidAI/LFM2.5-350M} and \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base}) only when cheaper rungs abstain or require exact target verification.
  \item \textbf{Downward \texttt{SleepCrystallizer}}: Extracts recurring wake traces using Walsh--Hadamard spectral warm-starts, joint-mode minterm truth tables, and Waksman Bene\v{s} switch schedules, hot-swapping shadow BRAM banks without halting inference or running FPGA place-and-route.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{0.1 Contributions}\label{contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Amortized downward compilation condition (Section 2.1)}: Extends the Simon--Kadane $p_i / c_i$ cascade ordering with an explicit crystallization cost $E_{\text{compile}}$, establishing when compiling certified Rung 3 wakes into Rung 0 \texttt{LUT6} pages lowers cumulative energy-to-correct-completion.
  \item \textbf{Non-blocking $512 \times 512$ Bene\v{s}--Copy--Bene\v{s} + \texttt{81}-\texttt{RAMB36E2} RTL architecture (Section 2.2, Section 3.3, Claim LA-3)}: Eliminates both the \texttt{102.0\%} KV260 LUT overflow of flat crossbars (\texttt{119,478} LUTs) and the \texttt{926,619}-LUT explosion of flip-flop page multiplexers, synthesizing to \texttt{41,186} \texttt{xcup} CLB LUTs (\texttt{35.2\%} of \texttt{xck26}), \texttt{81} \texttt{RAMB36E2}s (\texttt{56.2\%}), and \texttt{0} \texttt{DSP48} slices with \texttt{1}-cycle (\texttt{4.0 ns} at \texttt{250 MHz} nominal) page switching in simulation.
  \item \textbf{Universal $\le 64$-bit joint-mode minterm synthesis (Section 2.3, Claims LA-5 \& LA-7)}: Proves joint-mode minterm lookup dominates independent bit-plane DiffLogic argmax on correlated branches (\texttt{if/else}, BPE multi-token bursts) with zero off-support chimeras, packing $K = \lfloor 64 / \lceil \log_2 |V| \rceil \rfloor$ subword tokens into a single \texttt{uint64} (\texttt{16b $\times$ 4 = 64b} for $|V|=65{,}536$; \texttt{18b $\times$ 3 = 54b} for $|V|=248{,}320$).
  \item \textbf{Oracle-free Split-Conformal + Online ACI crystallization gate (Section 2.4, Claim LA-4)}: Pairs a quadratically regularized latent \texttt{JEPA-EBRM} score with Split Conformal calibration and Adaptive Conformal Inference (Gibbs and Cand\`es, 2021), achieving \texttt{0.00\%} false crystallization risk and \texttt{1.0000} OOD AUROC on shifted regimes where softmax MSP, margin, Helmholtz logit energy, and 5-pass MC-Dropout suffer \texttt{17.62\%--24.28\%} false risk.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{AirMoL} and \texttt{Expert-AirMoL} 4-bit NVMe streaming up to \texttt{34.66B} Sparse-MoE (Sections 3.4--3.5, Claims LA-6, LA-7, LA-9)}: Combines Darwin \texttt{F\_NOCACHE} \texttt{os.pread} 4-bit sharding, router-indexed \texttt{.experts.pack4} byte-stride expert \texttt{pread}, hybrid state rollback, and online token-\texttt{LUT6} burst speculation across October 2026 \texttt{LiquidAI/LFM3-1.7B} (\texttt{2.74$\times$} I/O cut in \texttt{3.79\%} RAM), \texttt{LiquidAI/LFM2.5-8B-A1B} (\texttt{8.47B}; \texttt{6.90$\times$} I/O cut), and \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B} (\texttt{34.66B}; \textbf{\texttt{12.30$\times$} I/O cut}, \textbf{\texttt{2.53 GB} RAM = \texttt{3.83\%} of FP16}, \textbf{\texttt{6.50$\times$} speedup}) at \texttt{100.0\%} exact token parity.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{Sub1Bit-LUT6} vs.\ \texttt{LittleBit} and verifier-gated late-2026 frontier coding cascade (Section 3.5, Claims LA-10 \& LA-11)}: Replaces the \texttt{1.98M} runtime binary MACs of \texttt{LittleBit-1 / LittleBit-2} (\texttt{0.576 bpw}; Lee et al., 2025; Lee and Kim, 2026) with zero-MAC \texttt{Sub1Bit-LUT6} partial-sum tables (\texttt{< 4e-6} error, \texttt{100.0\%} parity via \texttt{MoL} in \texttt{0} QAT epochs), and outperforms standalone \texttt{LittleBit-2 0.55bpw}, \texttt{LFM3-1.7B}, \texttt{Gemma-4-E2B-it}, \texttt{LFM2.5-8B-A1B}, \texttt{Qwen3.6-35B-A3B}, and \texttt{RouteLLM} on a \texttt{48}-task (\texttt{16} specs $\times$ \texttt{3} waves) benchmark (\texttt{43.75\%} vs.\ \texttt{0.00\%--33.33\%}) with \textbf{\texttt{0.00\%} broken commits admitted} and \textbf{\texttt{35,590$\times$} Wave-3 crystallized speedup}, backed by five-language polyglot verification (Claims LA-2 \& LA-8).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{0.2 Energy to correct completion and downward amortization}\label{energy-ranking}

Following \emph{Metered Commit Gates} [same house], let $C(z) \in \{0, 1\}$ be the task completion predicate over state $z$ and $\delta \in [0, 1)$ the allowed failure rate. Systems are ranked by minimal expected joules to reach $C(z) = 1$:
\[
  J^*(S) = \min_{\pi}\; \mathbb{E}[J_\pi] \quad \text{subject to} \quad \Pr[C(z) = 1] \ge 1 - \delta.
\]
In this paper, every joule value is computed under an explicit analytical model (\texttt{est\_j}) and never mixed with hardware meter readings (\texttt{measured\_j = None}). Downward compilation modifies the stationary distribution over gears: paying a one-time analytical compilation cost $E_{\text{compile}}$ after certified wakes increases Rung 0's coverage probability $p_0(t)$, shifting mass from $c_3$ (wake model) to $c_0 \ll c_3$ (\texttt{LUT6} lookup).

\subsection{0.3 Relation to OpenIE Mixture of Limits and RCE-JEPA [same house]}\label{same-house}

Prior OpenIE studies --- \emph{Metered Commit Gates}, \emph{Task Satiation vs.\ Aggregate Rebound}, \emph{The Reality Code Engine}, \emph{RCE-JEPA}, and \emph{RCE Compile} --- are \texttt{[same house]} works cited for architectural continuity, never as independent external confirmation. Two invariants from those studies bind every module in \texttt{lut-adapt-ai}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Provenance non-strengthening (\texttt{ModelGenerated $\nrightarrow$ Deterministic})}: When \texttt{SleepCrystallizer} compiles \texttt{LUT6} truth tables from a deterministic \texttt{RCE} check (Section 4.1), the resulting table is \texttt{Check-safe} on verified holdout grids (guarded online by an \texttt{HC-SOINN} / \texttt{RCE} circuit breaker). When \texttt{SleepCrystallizer} compiles under the oracle-free \texttt{JEPA-EBRM} Split-Conformal + ACI gate (Section 2.4), the resulting table carries a finite-sample statistical coverage bound (\texttt{Conformal-bounded}, $1-\alpha$) and is \textbf{never} stamped \texttt{Deterministic}.
  \item \textbf{Proposer vs.\ authority in speculative decoding (\emph{The drafter proposes; the target decides})}: In byte-level and BPE token-level speculative decoding (Claims LA-5, LA-6, LA-7), \texttt{PagedBenesLUT6} acts strictly as a zero-FLOP proposer. Every emitted token is verified against the exact greedy distribution of the target model (\texttt{Mamba3CodeLM}, \texttt{LFM2.5-350M}, or \texttt{Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base}), guaranteeing \texttt{100.0\%} lossless output parity.
\end{itemize}

\section{2. Mathematical formulations and four results}\label{math-results}

\subsection{2.1 Result 1: Cascade ordering with amortized downward compilation}\label{result-1}

Consider gears $i \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ tried sequentially on an input $x$. Gear $i$ closes the task ($C(z)=1$) with probability $p_i$ at expected analytical energy $c_i > 0$. By Simon and Kadane (1975) (derived for Mixture of Limits in \emph{Metered Commit Gates}, Section II.2 [same house]), the expected energy of a permutation $\sigma$ under independent close events is
\[
  \mathbb{E}[J_\sigma] = \sum_{k=1}^{n} c_{\sigma(k)} \prod_{j < k} \bigl(1 - p_{\sigma(j)}\bigr),
\]
which is minimized if and only if gears are ordered by non-increasing ratio $p_{\sigma(1)}/c_{\sigma(1)} \ge \dots \ge p_{\sigma(n)}/c_{\sigma(n)}$.

Now suppose a stationary regime $\mathcal{D}_r$ persists for a remaining horizon of $\Delta T$ queries. Initially Rung 0 (\texttt{LUT6}) has coverage $p_0 = 0$ on $\mathcal{D}_r$, so every query escalates to a wake path of expected cost $c_{\text{wake}}$ (for example Rung 3 at cost $c_3$). After collecting $N_{\text{wake}}$ certified wake pairs $(x, y^*)$, \texttt{SleepCrystallizer} expends one-time compilation energy $E_{\text{compile}}$ to synthesize a Rung 0 \texttt{LUT6} page with certified coverage $p_0' \in (0, 1]$ and per-query lookup cost $c_0 \ll c_{\text{wake}}$.

\textbf{Proposition 1 (Net energy condition for downward crystallization).} \emph{Assuming the wake path closes the task ($p_{\text{wake}} = 1$), downward compilation strictly reduces expected cumulative spend over the remaining stationary horizon $\Delta T$ if and only if}
\[
  \Delta T \cdot \bigl(p_0' c_{\text{wake}} - c_0\bigr) > E_{\text{compile}}, \qquad \text{and} \qquad \frac{p_0'}{c_0} > \frac{1}{c_{\text{wake}}}.
\]
\emph{Proof.} Without crystallization, the remaining $\Delta T$ queries each incur expected energy $c_{\text{wake}}$, totaling $\Delta T \cdot c_{\text{wake}}$. With crystallization at one-time cost $E_{\text{compile}}$, Rung 0 precedes the wake path whenever $p_0'/c_0 > 1/c_{\text{wake}} \iff c_0 < p_0' c_{\text{wake}}$. Each subsequent query tries Rung 0 at cost $c_0$ and escalates with probability $1 - p_0'$, incurring expected energy $c_0 + (1 - p_0') c_{\text{wake}}$ and saving $p_0' c_{\text{wake}} - c_0 > 0$ per query. Summing over $\Delta T$ steps yields net cumulative savings $\Delta T(p_0' c_{\text{wake}} - c_0) - E_{\text{compile}} > 0$. When $c_0 / c_{\text{wake}} < 10^{-4}$, the threshold simplifies to $\Delta T \cdot p_0' c_{\text{wake}} > E_{\text{compile}}$. $\blacksquare$

\textbf{Consequence on non-stationary drift.} If regimes churn faster than $\Delta T^* = E_{\text{compile}} / (p_0' c_{\text{wake}} - c_0)$, compiling \texttt{LUT6} pages loses energy. \texttt{SleepCrystallizer} therefore gates compilation behind a minimum certified buffer $N_{\text{wake}}$ and holdout agreement $\tau_{\text{agree}} = 0.985$.

\subsection{2.2 Result 2: Non-blocking \texorpdfstring{$512 \times 512$}{512x512} Bene\v{s}--Copy--Bene\v{s} multicast routing}\label{result-2}

A Rung 0 layer of $G = 80$ dynamic \texttt{LUT6} primitives requires $M = 6G = 480$ input pins drawn from an $N = 512$-bit input bus. Because learned Boolean features exhibit variable fan-out (one input wire may feed zero \texttt{LUT6} pins or dozens), a standard $N \times N$ permutation Bene\v{s} network cannot route multicast assignments, while a flat $M \times N$ multiplexer crossbar requires $480$ independent $512:1$ muxes (\texttt{119,478} CLB LUTs already at $384$ ports, overflowing the \texttt{117,120}-LUT Kria KV260 \texttt{xck26}; Claim LA-3).

\textbf{Theorem 2 (Contention-free multicast via Bene\v{s}--Copy--Bene\v{s}).} \emph{Let $N = 2^n$ ($n=9, N=512$) and $M \le N$ ($M=480$). Any arbitrary many-to-one multicast mapping $\phi: \{0, \dots, M-1\} \to \{0, \dots, N-1\}$ is realizable without wire contention in a three-cascade $2 \times 2$ switch fabric $\mathcal{B}_1 \to \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{B}_2$ of depth}
\[
  D(N) = 2(2\log_2 N - 1) + \log_2 N = 5\log_2 N - 2 = 43 \text{ stages}
\]
\emph{comprising $43 \times (N/2) = 11{,}008$ SRAM-configured $2 \times 2$ cells, with $O(N \log N)$ configuration runtime and zero FPGA place-and-route.}

\emph{Proof.} Let $U = \{s_1, \dots, s_r\}$ be the $r \le M$ distinct active sources in the image of $\phi$ with fan-out multiplicities $m_1, \dots, m_r \ge 1$ summing to $\sum_{j=1}^r m_j = M \le N$. Define partial sums $S_0 = 0$ and $S_j = \sum_{i=1}^j m_i$.
(1) \emph{Concentration ($\mathcal{B}_1$, $2n-1 = 17$ stages)}: Choose any bijection $\pi_1$ on $\{0,\dots,N-1\}$ mapping $\pi_1(s_j) = S_{j-1}$ for $j=1,\dots,r$. By the Bene\v{s}--Waksman theorem (Bene\v{s}, 1965; Waksman, 1968), $\pi_1$ decomposes into $2n-1$ stages of $N/2$ $2\times 2$ switches via $O(N \log N)$ bipartite 2-coloring.
(2) \emph{Contiguous fan-out ($\mathcal{C}$, $n = 9$ stages)}: Replicating each seed at $S_{j-1}$ across the contiguous interval $[S_{j-1}, S_j - 1]$ defines disjoint monotonic intervals $[S_0, S_1-1], \dots, [S_{r-1}, S_r-1]$. By Thompson's monotonic copy network theorem (Thompson, 1978; Lee, 1988), binary-tree interval splitting across $n$ butterfly stages preserves strict endpoint monotonicity at every stage and routes all $M$ copies with zero link contention.
(3) \emph{Output permutation ($\mathcal{B}_2$, $2n-1 = 17$ stages)}: Map each contiguous block $[S_{j-1}, S_j-1]$ bijectively onto the target pin set $\phi^{-1}(s_j) \subset \{0,\dots,M-1\}$ and pad $[M, N-1]$ arbitrarily to form a bijection $\pi_2$, routed by Waksman's algorithm on $\mathcal{B}_2$. Composing $\pi_2 \circ \mathcal{C} \circ \pi_1$ delivers $\phi(q)$ to every pin $q \in \{0,\dots,M-1\}$. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{2.3 Result 3: Joint-mode minterm synthesis vs.\ marginal coordinate argmax}\label{result-3}

Standard differentiable LUT layers (Petersen et al., 2022) predict multi-bit or multi-token outputs $\mathbf{Y} = (Y_1, \dots, Y_B) \in \mathcal{Y}$ via independent binary gates $\hat{y}_b(x) = \mathbb{I}[\Pr(Y_b = 1 \mid \psi(x)) > 1/2]$ on each bit-plane $b \in \{1,\dots,B\}$. On multi-modal conditional distributions --- such as code after \texttt{if (} vs.\ \texttt{return }, or $K$-token BPE bursts --- independent bit-plane rounding creates invalid \textbf{chimera} tokens.

\textbf{Proposition 3 (Dominance and zero-chimera property of joint-mode minterms).} \emph{For any discrete context map $\psi: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{C}$ and discrete burst space $\mathcal{Y}$ encoded into $B \le 64$ bits, define the joint-mode minterm table $T^*(c) = \arg\max_{\mathbf{y} \in \mathcal{Y}} \Pr(\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{y} \mid \psi(X) = c)$ and the marginal bit-plane predictor $\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{\text{marg}}(c) = (\arg\max_{v \in \{0,1\}} \Pr(Y_b = v \mid \psi(X)=c))_{b=1}^B$. Then:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{$\Pr[\mathbf{Y} = T^*(\psi(X))] \ge \Pr[\mathbf{Y} = \hat{\mathbf{y}}^{\text{marg}}(\psi(X))]$, with strict inequality whenever marginal bit majorities disagree with the joint mode.}
  \item \emph{Every output of $T^*(c)$ belongs to $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{Y} \mid \psi(X)=c)$ (zero chimera bursts), whereas $\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{\text{marg}}(c)$ can achieve \texttt{0\%} exact-burst accuracy.}
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Proof.} Pointwise for each context $c$, $\Pr(\mathbf{Y} = g(c) \mid c) \le \max_{\mathbf{y} \in \mathcal{Y}} \Pr(\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{y} \mid c) = \Pr(\mathbf{Y} = T^*(c) \mid c)$ for every deterministic $g(c)$; taking expectations gives (1), and positive probability mass at the mode gives $T^*(c) \in \operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{Y}\mid c)$. For the counterexample in (2), let $B=3$ and place conditional mass $0.40, 0.35, 0.25$ on $(1,1,0), (1,0,1), (0,1,1)$: every marginal bit satisfies $\Pr(Y_b=1\mid c) \ge 0.60 > 1/2$, so $\hat{\mathbf{y}}^{\text{marg}}(c) = (1,1,1) \notin \operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{Y}\mid c)$ (\texttt{0\%} exact match vs.\ \texttt{40\%} for $T^*(c)=(1,1,0)$). $\blacksquare$

\textbf{Universal 64-bit BPE token packing.} For any pretrained tokenizer of vocabulary size $|V|$, each token requires $b_V = \lceil \log_2 |V| \rceil$ bits, packing $K = \lfloor 64 / b_V \rfloor$ tokens losslessly into one \texttt{uint64} word: \texttt{LFM2.5-350M} ($|V|=65{,}536, b_V=16 \implies K=4$, \texttt{64} bits) and \texttt{Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base} ($|V|=248{,}320, b_V=18 \implies K=3$, \texttt{54} bits $\le 64$).

\subsection{2.4 Result 4: Oracle-free latent \texttt{JEPA-EBRM} Split-Conformal + Online ACI gate}\label{result-4}

In open-world perceptual or telemetry tasks without a deterministic \texttt{RCE} check, gating \texttt{SleepCrystallizer} by softmax Maximum Softmax Probability (MSP) or logit energy (Liu et al., 2020) suffers from high-confidence Extrapolation far from training data (Hein et al., 2019), crystallizing hallucinated labels into \texttt{LUT6} banks (\texttt{17.6\%--24.3\%} false risk in Claim LA-4). We define a quadratically regularized latent \texttt{JEPA-EBRM} nonconformity score over encoder $z = f_\theta(x) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and class prototypes $\{\mu_y\}$:
\[
  E_\phi(x, y) = \frac{1}{2}\|f_\theta(x) - \mu_y\|_2^2 + \frac{\lambda_r}{2}\|f_\theta(x)\|_2^2 + \psi_\phi(f_\theta(x), y), \qquad \lambda_r > 0, \quad |\psi_\phi(z,y)| \le C_\psi.
\]
Calibration on $n$ exchangeable held-out pairs sets $\hat{Q}_{1-\alpha} = \operatorname{Quantile}_{\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha) \rceil / n}(\{E_\phi(X_i, Y_i)\}_{i=1}^n)$. Online, Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI; Gibbs and Cand\`es, 2021) updates $\alpha_{t+1} = \alpha_t + \gamma(\alpha - \text{err}_t)$ and forms $\mathcal{C}_t(x) = \{y : E_\phi(x, y) \le \hat{Q}_{1-\alpha_t}\}$. \texttt{SleepCrystallizer} compiles $(x, \hat{y})$ if and only if $|\mathcal{C}_t(x)| = 1$, stamping provenance \texttt{Conformal-bounded} (\texttt{ModelGenerated $\nrightarrow$ Deterministic}).

\textbf{Proposition 4 (Coverage, adaptive shift recovery, and radial OOD abstention).}
\emph{(1) Under exchangeable $(X_{\text{test}}, Y_{\text{test}})$ at fixed $\alpha_t=\alpha$, $\Pr[Y_{\text{test}} \in \mathcal{C}(X_{\text{test}})] \ge 1 - \alpha$ (Vovk et al., 2005; Lei et al., 2018). (2) Under arbitrary bounded shift with $\alpha_t \in (0,1)$, ACI satisfies $\lim_{T\to\infty} T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T \text{err}_t = \alpha$ (Gibbs and Cand\`es, 2021). (3) Because $|\psi_\phi| \le C_\psi$ and $\lambda_r > 0$, $\min_y E_\phi(x,y) \ge \frac{\lambda_r}{2}\|z\|_2^2 - C_\psi \to +\infty$ as $\|z\|_2 \to \infty$, so every ray leaving radius $R(\alpha_t) = \sqrt{2(\hat{Q}_{1-\alpha_t} + C_\psi)/\lambda_r}$ yields $\mathcal{C}_t(x) = \emptyset$ (explicit \texttt{Refuse} / abstain).} $\blacksquare$

\section{6. Limits, threats to validity, not claimed, and falsifiers}\label{limits}

\textbf{Internal validity.} Downward crystallization pays off only when regime persistence $\Delta T$ exceeds Proposition 1's amortization threshold. On i.i.d.\ random bitstreams or per-step adversarial churn ($\Delta T = 1$), $p_0' \approx 0$ and the system falls back to \texttt{1.00 tok/sweep} plus Rung 0 lookup overhead. On \texttt{Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base} cold prompts (\texttt{Claim LA-7}), CPU verification of $K+1 = 4$ tokens reduced wall-clock speed (\texttt{0.32} vs.\ \texttt{0.53 tok/s}) despite cutting NVMe bytes (\texttt{8.581} vs.\ \texttt{12.674 GB}), showing that CPU batch verification requires post-wake draft acceptance (\texttt{2.74 tok/sweep} $\to$ \texttt{1.11 tok/s}) or GPU/FPGA offload to beat \texttt{T=1} latency.

\textbf{Construct validity.} \texttt{100.0\%} lossless parity guarantees bit-for-bit agreement with greedy target decoding; it does not raise the underlying capability ceiling of a \texttt{350M} or \texttt{0.8B} checkpoint. Likewise, \texttt{Conformal-bounded} ($1-\alpha$) bounds statistical miscoverage on the calibrated \texttt{JEPA-EBRM} manifold and never promotes neural proposals to \texttt{Deterministic}.

\textbf{External validity.} Foundation-model \texttt{AirMoL}, \texttt{Expert-AirMoL}, and coding bakeoff runs were measured across six 2026 checkpoints (\texttt{LFM2.5-350M}, \texttt{Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base}, \texttt{LiquidAI/LFM3-1.7B}, \texttt{google/gemma-4-E2B-it}, \texttt{LiquidAI/LFM2.5-8B-A1B} \texttt{8.47B}, and \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B} \texttt{34.66B}, plus \texttt{LittleBit-2} \texttt{Qwen3-8B} \texttt{0.55 bpw}) on Apple M5 Max with external NVMe storage. Checkpoints at \texttt{70B+} remain labeled projections in \texttt{air\_mol\_nvme\_baselines.csv}.

\textbf{Measurement validity.} Per-task \texttt{J/VC}, \texttt{est\_j/tok}, and \texttt{mJ/byte} values are analytical estimates (\texttt{est\_j}), never isolated silicon rail power (\texttt{measured\_j = None}), while macOS \texttt{PMU} cumulative battery/adapter deltas include whole-system OS/SSD/display load. Yosys \texttt{0.71+} and Verilator \texttt{5.054} prove structural \texttt{xck26} synthesis and cycle-accurate \texttt{CPI = 1} behavior, not Vivado post-PAR \texttt{250 MHz} timing closure or KV260 rail watts (\texttt{board\_metered = false}).

\textbf{Not claimed.} Not claimed here: physical KV260 bitstream loading or rail-metered joules for \texttt{clai\_benes\_hotswap\_core.sv}; Vivado post-PAR timing closure at \texttt{250 MHz}; silicon energy leadership over commercial ASICs; promotion of \texttt{ModelGenerated} outputs to \texttt{Deterministic}; or full repository-scale \texttt{SWE-bench} evaluation beyond the \texttt{48}-task contract benchmark in Claim LA-11.
